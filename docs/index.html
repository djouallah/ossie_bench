One semantic model, three platforms

One model with two facts at two grains (daily sales, monthly budget), built natively on Power BI, Snowflake and Databricks, then asked the same 13 questions. Every platform can reach every answer. What differs is who does the work: the engine, the model, or the person writing the query.

The idea: drill across

Kimball's rule for a question that touches more than one table: group each table on its own, down to the dimension they share, then merge the results on that dimension. Never join the tables first.

The smallest example in this repo: days in the calendar and sales, by year. The calendar runs to 2023. Sales stop at 2022.

Drill across

Two group-bys, then a merge.

dim_calendar1,461 rows, 2020 to 2023
GROUP BY year
fact_sales2,411,200 rows, 2020 to 2022
GROUP BY year
merge on year (full outer join)
4 rows. 2023 is there: 365 days, no sales.

Single pass

One join, then one group-by.

fact_sales LEFT JOIN dim_calendar one row per sale: 2,411,200 rows, none of them dated 2023
GROUP BY year
3 rows. 2023 is gone, and nothing says so. The day count is the days that had a sale, which happens to equal the calendar here.

The same move with two facts

Actual against budget by category, 2021. Sales are daily by store and product; the budget is monthly by country and category. Each is summed on its own, then merged on category. The percentage is computed after the merge, from the two totals.

fact_salesgrouped by category, through product
fact_budgetgrouped by category, its own column
merged on categorythen (sales - budget) / budget

How each platform does it

The same question, days and sales by year, asked as plainly as each platform allows. The steps under Snowflake and Databricks are read from their own query plans.

Power BI

1 model · 7 tables · 16 measures

Who drills across

The engine
  1. The groups come from the column asked for, dim_calendar[year]: four years.
  2. Each expression is evaluated per year against its own table: the day count on the calendar, the sales measure on the fact, filtered through the relationship.
  3. A year is kept when any expression has a value, so 2023 stays with a blank for sales.

From how SUMMARIZECOLUMNS is defined. Power BI shows no SQL plan for this.

Snowflake

1 semantic view · 16 logical tables · 19 metrics

Who drills across

The engine
  1. Aggregate the calendar by year: COUNT(date_key).
  2. Aggregate the sales by date, join them to the calendar, aggregate again by year: SUM(sales_qty).
  3. FullOuterJoin the two results on year.

From EXPLAIN on the query below. The semantic view compiles to drill-across.

Databricks

5 metric views · one source each · 20 measures

Who drills across

You, in the query
  1. Scan fact_sales, the source of the metric view.
  2. LeftOuter join the calendar onto it. Still one row per sale.
  3. One GroupingAgg by year over that rowset computes both columns.

From EXPLAIN FORMATTED. The calendar is only reachable through a sale, so a year without sales has no rows.

Drill-across by hand on Databricks

The calendar gets its own metric view. The query groups each view on its own and merges the two, which is the plan Snowflake builds for you.

The Apache Ossie draft (0.3.0.dev0) writes drill-across down as the rule for any engine: metrics are evaluated in branches, one per home dataset, and the branches are combined on the dimension keys. wide_table_semantics.md, sections 5.4 and 5.5.

A metric view is one grain

A metric view is one source table with its dimensions joined on, so everything in it lives at the grain of that source. That is why 2023 disappears above, and it is also why "last year" behaves the way it does. Each platform defines it differently:

Power BI: move the filter

CALCULATE (
  [sales_quantity],
  SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR ( dim_calendar[date_key] )
)

The dates in the current group are shifted a year, then the measure is evaluated. It works at any grain because the group decides which dates are shifted.

Snowflake: another branch

sales_ly AS FACT_SALES  -- a second logical table
sales_ly.sale_date_ly AS DATEADD('year', 1, sale_date)
sales_ly.sales_qty_ly AS SUM(sales_ly.sales_qty)

A copy of the fact with its date moved a year, related to the calendar through the moved date. "Last year" is one more table to drill across, so it also works at any grain.

Databricks: a window

- name: sales_qty_ly
  expr: SUM(sales_qty)
  window:
  - order: date_key
    range: current
    offset: -1 year
    semiadditive: last
  # then one entry per other calendar column

A window ordered by one column, the date. Asked by date it is right. Asked by month, each month gets one day's value, with no error.

Sales last year, as each route returned it. Marked cells differ from the reference.

A measure per grain fixes the months and still gets the total wrong. The route that matches is a second metric view of the fact with its date moved a year, joined in the query: Snowflake's extra branch, done by hand.
